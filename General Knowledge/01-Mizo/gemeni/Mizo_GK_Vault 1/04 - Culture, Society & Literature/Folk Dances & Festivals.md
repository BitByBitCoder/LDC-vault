# Folk Dances & Traditional Festivals

Mizo festivals are called **Kut**, traditional agricultural events associated with stages of *Jhum* (slash-and-burn) farming.

---

## 🌾 The Three Major Kuts

### 1. Chapchar Kut (The Spring Festival)
- **Timing:** March (after clearing forests for *Jhum* cultivation).
- **Significance:** The most important and grandest Mizo festival.
- **Highlights:** Mass dancing of [[#Cheraw (Bamboo Dance)]], *Chheihlam*, traditional songs, and cultural dress (*Puan*).

### 2. Mim Kut (Maize Harvest Festival)
- **Timing:** August / September (after harvesting maize).
- **Significance:** Solemn festival dedicated to honoring departed souls (*Mithi Khua*). Offers fresh maize, vegetables, and bread to ancestor spirits.

### 3. Pawl Kut (Harvest Festival)
- **Timing:** December (after completing the main grain/paddy harvest).
- **Significance:** Thanksgiving festival celebrated with feasts, fun, and games.

---

## 💃 Traditional Mizo Dances

| Dance Name | Description / Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cheraw** | Famous **Bamboo Dance**. Dancers step expertly in and out between crossed bamboo staves rhythmically clapped together by rhythm keepers. |
| **Chheihlam** | Energetic folk dance accompanied by the beating of drums or gongs (*Khuang*) and traditional verse recitation (*Chheih Hla*). |
| **Khuallam** | "Dance of the Guests", performed in traditional costume (*Puanchei*) using woven shawls held outstretched. |
| **Solakia / Sarlamkai** | Traditional warrior dances of the Mara and Pawi (Lai) tribes celebrating victory in battle. |
| **Tlanglam** | Popular group dance performed to traditional or contemporary songs. |

---

## 👗 Traditional Textiles (*Puan*)
- **Puanchei:** Highly ornate woven garment worn by women during festivals.
- **Kawrchei:** Traditional woven blouse.
- **Ntekherh / Tawlhloh Puan:** Status/warrior traditional cloths.
