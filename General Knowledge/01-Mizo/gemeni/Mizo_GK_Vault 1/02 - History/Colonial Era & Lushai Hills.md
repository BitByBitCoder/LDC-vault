# Colonial Era & Lushai Hills District

## ⚔️ British Expeditions & Annexation
During the 19th century, Mizo chiefs conducted raids into British tea plantations in Cachar, Sylhet, and Chittagong. 

> [!HISTORICAL-EVENT] The Changsil Raid & Winchester Incident (1871)
> In 1871, Mizo raiders raided Alexandria tea garden in Cachar, killing James Winchester and capturing his young daughter **Mary Winchester** (*Zosangliani*). This led to the major **Lushai Expedition of 1871–1872**.

### Key Chronology:
- **1871–1872:** First major British military expedition into Lushai Hills.
- **1889–1890:** Chin-Lushai Expedition leads to firm British control.
- **1890:** Fort Aijal (Aizawl) and Fort Lungleh (Lunglei) established.
- **1898:** North Lushai Hills (under Assam) and South Lushai Hills (under Bengal) merged to form the **Lushai Hills District** under the Chief Commissioner of Assam.
- **First Superintendent of Lushai Hills:** Major J. Shakespear (1898).

---

## ✝️ Arrival of Christian Missionaries
The entry of Christian missionaries transformed Mizo literacy, healthcare, and religion.

- **January 11, 1894:** Arrival of Pioneer Missionaries **Rev. F.W. Savidge** (*Sap Upa*) and **Rev. J.H. Lorrain** (*Pu Buanga*) at Aizawl.
  - They created the Mizo written language using the **Roman script**.
  - Prepared the first Mizo grammar and dictionary.
  - Opened the first formal school in Aizawl (1898).
- **Welsh Presbyterian Mission:** Established by Rev. D.E. Jones in 1897.
- **Baptist Missionary Society (BMS):** Focused on Southern Mizoram (Lunglei) from 1903.

---

## 📜 Administrative Acts
- **Excluded Area:** Under the Government of India Act 1935, Lushai Hills was designated an "Excluded Area", keeping it outside general provincial legislation.
- **Abolition of Chieftainship:** The *Assam-Lushai Hills District (Abolition of Chief's Rights) Act, 1954* formally ended the traditional *Lal* system.
