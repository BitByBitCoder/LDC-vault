# Pre-Colonial History & Traditional Chieftainship

## 📜 Origins & Migration
The Mizo people belong to the **Mongoloid race** and speak a language belonging to the **Tibeto-Burman family**. 
- According to Mizo oral traditions and folklore, the ancestors originated from a mythical cave known as **Chhinlung** (or *Sinlung*).
- Migration path: From Central Asia $\rightarrow$ China $\rightarrow$ Myanmar (Shan State & Kabaw Valley) $\rightarrow$ Chin Hills $\rightarrow$ Present-day Mizoram (around 16th–18th Century).

---

## 👑 The Chieftainship System (*Lal*)
Before British annexation, the Mizo society was governed by autocratic village chiefs called **Lal**.

> [!KEY-CONCEPT] Autocracy and Responsibility of the Lal
> - The chief was the supreme owner of all land within his domain.
> - The chief was assisted by a council of elders called **Upas**.
> - In return for protection, villagers paid tribute in grain (**Buhchhun**) and meat (**Sathang**).

### Key Officials in Traditional Village Administration:
1. **Upas:** Elders appointed by the chief to assist in administration and judicial decisions.
2. **Tlangau:** The village crier/announcer who disseminated orders.
3. **Zalen:** Wealthy villagers exempted from tax in exchange for helping the chief during emergencies.
4. **Puithiam:** The village priest who performed sacrifices and religious rites.
5. **Thirdeng:** The village blacksmith responsible for repairing weapons and agricultural tools.

---

## 🏠 The *Zawlbuk* (Bachelors' Dormitory)
The **Zawlbuk** was the center of Mizo youth life, social organization, and defense.
- Located at the center of the village near the chief's house.
- Trained young men in physical endurance, wrestling, village defense, hunting, and moral values (*Tlawmngaihna*).
- Abolished gradually in the 1930s–1940s as formal education and modern social organizations (like YMA - Young Mizo Association) emerged.
