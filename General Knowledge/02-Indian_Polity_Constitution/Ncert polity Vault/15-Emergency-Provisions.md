# Emergency Provisions (Articles 352–360)

| Question                                                                                               |                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Under which Article can National Emergency be proclaimed, on what grounds?                             | Article 352; war, external aggression, armed rebellion, or threat thereof          |
| On whose recommendation must the President act to declare National Emergency?                          | Written recommendation of the Cabinet                                              |
| Within how long must Parliament approve the proclamation, with what majority?                          | 1 month, with special majority in both Houses                                      |
| Once approved, how long does the proclamation last before needing renewal?                             | A fresh 6-month lease each time it's approved                                      |
| Can Parliament extend Lok Sabha's normal life during emergency?                                        | Yes, 1 year at a time, not exceeding 6 months after emergency ends                 |
| When was Lok Sabha's term extended, and by the 42nd Amendment context?                                 | Only once, in 1976                                                                 |
| Can Parliament legislate on State subjects during National Emergency?                                  | Yes                                                                                |
| Effect of Emergency on Article 19 rights, under which Article?                                         | Article 358 — Art 19 rights become non-existent against the State during emergency |
| Effect of Emergency on right to move courts for FR enforcement, under which Article?                   | Article 359 — can be suspended by Presidential Order                               |
| Which Articles' rights CANNOT be suspended even during emergency?                                      | Articles 20 and 21                                                                 |
| When was the FIRST National Emergency proclaimed, and why?                                             | 26 October 1962, due to Chinese aggression in NEFA                                 |
| Under which Article/name is "President's Rule" (State Emergency) declared, on what ground?             | Article 356, for failure of constitutional machinery in a State                    |
| Under Art 352 emergency, how does Parliament legislate on State subjects — directly or via delegation? | Directly, by itself                                                                |
| Under Art 356 emergency, can Parliament delegate its power?                                            | Yes — to the State Legislature, the President, or another authority                |
| Maximum period a President's Rule (Art 356) proclamation can be extended by Parliament?                | Up to 3 years                                                                      |
| Under which Article is Financial Emergency declared, on what ground?                                   | Article 360, threat to India's financial stability or credit                       |

## Exam Trap ⚠️
- Article 358 vs 359: 358 = automatic suspension of Art 19 during National Emergency (no separate order needed); 359 = Presidential ORDER needed to suspend right to move courts for OTHER Fundamental Rights.
- Art 20 (protection re: conviction) and Art 21 (life & personal liberty) are the ONLY FRs that survive suspension even during emergency — a very frequently tested fact.
- Three types: National (352 – war/aggression/rebellion), State/President's Rule (356 – constitutional machinery failure), Financial (360 – financial stability threat). Only National Emergency has been invoked as "external aggression" (1962, 1971) and "internal disturbance/armed rebellion" (1975).
