# 01 Constitutional vs Non-Constitutional Bodies

| Question                                                 | Answer                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What are Constitutional Bodies?                          | \|\|Bodies whose establishment is mentioned in various Articles of the Constitution\|\| |
| Where do Constitutional Bodies derive their powers from? | \|\|Directly from the Constitution\|\|                                                  |
| What are Non-Constitutional Bodies?                      | \|\|Bodies not mentioned in the Constitution\|\|                                        |
| Who proposes a Non-Constitutional Body?                  | \|\|Cabinet\|\|                                                                         |
| How are they constituted?                                | \|\|By an Act of Parliament / State Legislation\|\|                                     |

| Feature | Constitutional Body | Non-Constitutional Body |
|---|---|---|
| Mentioned in Constitution | Yes | No |
| Source of power | Directly the Constitution | Act of Parliament / State legislation (or Cabinet proposal) |
| Examples | ECI, UPSC, SPSC, CAG, Attorney General, GST Council, NCSC, NCST, NCBC, Linguistic Minorities Commissioner | NHRC, CIC, CVC, CBI, NCW, National Commission for Minorities, Lokpal, NITI Aayog |

> [!warning] Exam Trap ⚠️
> - **NCBC** was statutory (1993) and became constitutional only through the **102nd CAA, 2018** (Art. 338B).
> - **NITI Aayog** is **non-constitutional AND non-statutory**; NHRC/CIC/CVC/NCW/Lokpal are statutory.
> - **Tribunals** were not in the original Constitution - added by the 42nd Amendment.
