# Personal Constitution

  

*Rules to hold from now until the last bed. Organized under the four cardinal virtues — Wisdom, Courage, Justice, Temperance. Review, don't just read.*

  

---

  

## I. Wisdom (Sophia)

  

1. Separate what's in your control from what isn't, every single time, before you react.

2. First principles before opinions — rebuild your view from what you can verify, not from what you inherited.

3. The map is not the territory; hold your models loosely.

4. Ask "and then what?" twice before committing to any plan.

5. You don't rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems — audit the system, not the goal.

6. Strong opinions, weakly held; update the moment the evidence moves.

7. Most suffering comes from the story about the event, not the event itself.

8. Know the difference between a fact, an inference, and a feeling — and label them honestly to yourself.

9. Read widely outside your field; the best insights are usually imports.

10. If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet.

11. Study your own biology before judging your own behavior — context and chemistry shape more than willpower does.

12. Complexity hides in feedback loops — look for what's reinforcing itself before blaming a single cause.

13. Small initial conditions produce large downstream differences — take your first hour of the day seriously.

14. Write your reasoning down; a thought unexamined on paper is usually less rigorous than it feels in your head.

15. Silence and solitude are instruments of thought, not punishments — schedule them.

16. Question the question before you answer it.

17. Every expert was once a beginner who kept showing up — impatience with your own learning curve is not wisdom.

18. Seek out the smartest disagreement you can find, not the loudest agreement.

19. The obstacle in the way becomes the way — reframe blockers as material, not verdicts.

20. Know your own biases by name so they can't operate on you unnoticed.

21. A rule you can't explain to a stranger in one sentence is a rule you don't actually understand.

22. Death is not a distant event; let its certainty sharpen your priorities today, not sadden you.

23. Don't confuse motion with progress; check outcomes, not activity.

24. What you can't measure, you can still observe carefully — don't discard signal for false precision.

25. Teach what you learn immediately; explanation is the final exam of understanding.

  

## II. Courage (Andreia)

  

26. Say the true thing, especially when it costs you something to say it.

27. Do the hard conversation today; postponement is just interest compounding on your fear.

28. Fear is data, not a command — feel it, then decide anyway.

29. Take responsibility before you're caught, not after.

30. Fail loudly enough that you learn something, quietly enough that you don't perform the failure for an audience.

31. Act first — courage follows the action, it doesn't precede it.

32. Say no without a paragraph of justification.

33. Choose the harder right over the easier wrong, every time you notice the fork.

34. Ask for what you actually want; ambiguity is a form of cowardice.

35. Confront the problem while it's small; it only grows in the dark.

36. Public failure survives; a private life of avoidance does not.

37. Disagree with the room when you believe the room is wrong.

38. Set the boundary the first time it's crossed, not the fifth.

39. Being liked is not a strategy — being respected is a byproduct of being honest.

40. Walk toward the conversation you're avoiding; it's smaller up close than from a distance.

41. Take the risk that's reversible; save your caution for what actually can't be undone.

42. Correct your own mistake in public before someone else finds it.

43. Endure necessary discomfort without narrating it as tragedy.

44. Compete against your last version of yourself, not the room.

45. Don't let politeness override honesty when someone needs the truth.

46. Resolve conflicts directly — don't just absorb them and call it patience.

47. When in doubt, do the uncomfortable thing that moves you forward.

48. A reputation for candor is worth more than a reputation for comfort.

49. Start before you're ready; readiness is a myth used to postpone.

50. Stand alone on a principle before you abandon it for company.

  

## III. Justice (Dikaiosyne)

  

51. Leave every interaction slightly better than you found it, without needing credit for it.

52. Keep your word even when the other person would never find out you broke it.

53. Don't optimize your own outcome at a cost you wouldn't accept if someone you love had to pay it.

54. Give credit loudly, take blame quietly.

55. Treat the person with no power over you exactly as you treat the one who has all of it.

56. Pay debts — financial, favors, apologies — promptly and without being asked twice.

57. Speak of people the same way whether they're in the room or not.

58. Fairness is not the same as sameness — give people what they need, not just what's equal.

59. Protect the absent; don't let a reputation be built in someone's absence without a fair defense.

60. Your duty to your community doesn't end where your comfort begins.

61. Correct the record when you're wrong, even if no one's checking.

62. Don't outsource your ethics to "everyone else does it."

63. Mentor someone without expecting a return.

64. A promise made under pressure is still a promise.

65. Notice who's carrying an unequal share of the load and rebalance it if it's yours to rebalance.

66. Never let your success be built on someone else's uncredited work.

67. Hold the powerful and the powerless to the same standard you hold yourself.

68. Apologize specifically, not generally — name what you did, not just that you're sorry.

69. Participate in the systems that protect you even when it's inconvenient.

70. Loyalty to a person shouldn't override loyalty to the truth.

71. When you take, know what you owe back, and pay it.

72. Judge actions, not identities.

73. If you wouldn't want it printed with your name on it, don't do it privately either.

74. Make room at the table before you're asked to.

75. Accountability delayed on yourself is still accountability — hold yourself to it even late.

  

## IV. Temperance (Sophrosyne)

  

76. Master the urge before it masters the hour.

77. Eat, drink, and consume as if your future self is watching and has veto power.

78. Rest is a discipline, not a reward you have to earn.

79. Anger is a loan from your future calm — most of the time it's not worth the interest.

80. Practice discomfort voluntarily sometimes, so involuntary discomfort never catches you unprepared.

81. Enough is a decision, not a destination you arrive at automatically.

82. Guard your attention like it's the only truly non-renewable resource you have — because it is.

83. Delay the impulsive purchase, message, or reaction by one full day.

84. Moderate even your virtues; obsessive discipline is just another appetite in disguise.

85. Sleep on schedule; willpower is downstream of a rested brain, not the other way around.

86. Keep pleasure in proportion — the moment it starts running your calendar, it's running you.

87. Practice going without, occasionally, on purpose — comfort you can walk away from is the only comfort you truly own.

88. Speak less than you think you need to; most sentences improve with a cut.

89. Equanimity isn't numbness — it's choosing your response speed on purpose.

90. Don't let a good mood or a bad one make decisions that outlast the mood.

91. Train the body deliberately; discipline earned there transfers everywhere else.

92. Say less about your plans and more about your results.

93. Keep your wants few enough that losing one doesn't cost you your peace.

94. Practice a fast — from food, from noise, from your phone — regularly enough that none of them own you.

95. React to praise and criticism with the same even keel.

96. Simplicity in habits buys you bandwidth for the decisions that actually matter.

97. Never make a big decision hungry, furious, lonely, or exhausted.

98. Keep your environment cleaner than your willpower needs it to be — make the right path the easy path.

99. When you notice envy, treat it as information about what you actually want, then go build it.

100. Practice dying well by practicing letting go of small things daily — it's the same muscle.
