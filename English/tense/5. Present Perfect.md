### Formula Breakdown

In the present perfect, you always use **has** or **have** followed by the **past participle ($\text{V}_3$)** form of the verb.

| **Sentence Type** | **Formula**                                                                | **Example**                                                                                        |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Positive (+)**  | $\text{Subject} + \text{has / have} + \text{V}_3\text{ (Past Participle)}$ | _She **has finished** her work._<br><br>  <br><br>_They **have left** the office._                 |
| **Negative (-)**  | $\text{Subject} + \text{has / have} + \text{not} + \text{V}_3$             | _She **has not (hasn't) finished** her work._<br><br>  <br><br>_They **have not (haven't) left**._ |

**1.** Q: She -- her homework already. a) finish b) finished c) has finished d) is finishing **Identify:** "already" + no exact time → connects past to present. Subject "She" = singular → has + V3 → **has finished**

**2.** Q: I -- this movie before. a) see b) saw c) have seen d) am seeing **Identify:** "==before==" + no specific time → Present Perfect. Subject "I" → have + V3 → **have seen**

**3.** Q: Have you -- your lunch yet? a) eat b) ate c) eaten d) eating **Identify:** "yet" (in a question) = asking if something is done up to now → Present Perfect. After "have," you need V3 → **eaten**

**4.** Q: They -- to Delhi twice this year. a) go b) went c) have gone d) are going **Identify:** "this year" (still ongoing period) + no exact date → Present Perfect. Subject "They" = plural → have + V3 → **have gone**

**5.** Q: He -- here since 2018. a) live b) lived c) has lived d) is living **Identify:** "since" + a starting point still connected to now → Present Perfect. Subject "He" = singular → has + V3 → **has lived**

**6.** Q: We -- each other for ten years. a) know b) knew c) have known d) are knowing **Identify:** "for" + duration continuing to now → Present Perfect. Subject "We" = plural → have + V3 → **have known**

**7.** Q: I -- never -- such a beautiful place. a) have / seen b) has / seen c) had / seen d) am / seeing **Identify:** "never" = signal word for Present Perfect. Subject "I" → have + V3 → **have seen**

**8.** Q: She -- just -- the office. a) has / left b) have / left c) had / left d) is / leaving **Identify:** "just" = very recent action with present relevance → Present Perfect. Subject "She" = singular → has + V3 → **has left**

**9.** Q: -- you ever -- to Japan? a) Have / been b) Has / been c) Did / go d) Are / going **Identify:** "ever" = signal word asking about life experience up to now → Present Perfect. Subject "you" → Have + V3 → **have been**

**10.** Q: The train -- already. a) leave b) left c) has left d) is leaving **Identify:** "already" + result relevant now (you probably missed it) → Present Perfect. Subject "The train" (it) = singular → has + V3 → **has left**

---

**Pattern to lock in:**

- **have** → I, you, we, they (and all plurals)
- **has** → he, she, it (and singular nouns)
- Always followed by **V3** (past participle), never V2
- Signal words: already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for

**Key trap to watch for:** Present Perfect has no exact past time. If you see "yesterday," "last week," or "in 2015," it's Simple Past, not Present Perfect — even if the sentence "feels" similar.

- ❌ "I have finished it yesterday." (wrong — exact time given)
- ✅ "I finished it yesterday." (Simple Past)
- ✅ "I have already finished it." (Present Perfect — no exact time)
