The reason **"completed"** is incorrect comes down to a fundamental rule of English grammar: **you cannot use two past tense words together for a single action.**
### Why "did not completed" is Wrong

- **"Did"** = Past Tense
    
- **"Completed"** = Past Tense


### In English Grammar (Irregular Verbs)



- **Regular Verbs:** Follow a predictable rule. To make them past tense, you simply add **"-ed"**.
    
    - _walk_ $\rightarrow$ _walked_
        
    - _cook_ $\rightarrow$ _cooked_
        
    - _play_ $\rightarrow$ _played_
        
- **Irregular Verbs:** **Break the rules.** They do _not_ take "-ed" in the past tense. Instead, they change completely or stay the same:
    
    - _go_ $\rightarrow$ _went_ (completely changes)
        
    - _eat_ $\rightarrow$ _ate_ (changes spelling)
        
    - _buy_ $\rightarrow$ _bought_ (changes form)
        
    - _cut_ $\rightarrow$ _cut_ (doesn't change at all)

**Pattern to lock in:**

- Signal words: _yesterday, last week/month/year, ... ago, in [past year], when I was a child_
- Regular verbs: add **-ed** (watch → watched, study → studied)
- Irregular verbs: no fixed rule — you have to memorize them (go → went, buy → bought, leave → left, lose → lost)

**Key contrast so far:**

- "She **studies** English" (habit, Simple Present)
- "She **is studying** English right now" (in progress, Present Continuous)
- "She **studied** English in 2015" (finished, specific past time, Simple Past)

You _can_ use irregular verbs — you just can't add "-ed" to them. They have their own special past form that doesn't follow a rule, so you have to memorize each one individually.

## The two types of verbs in Simple Past

**Regular verbs** — follow a rule: add **-ed**

- watch → watch**ed**
- study → stud**ied**
- call → call**ed**
- serve → serv**ed**

**Irregular verbs** — no rule, random past forms you must memorize

- go → **went**
- buy → **bought**
- leave → **left**
- lose → **lost**
- eat → **ate**
- see → **saw**
- come → **came**
- take → **took**
- give → **gave**
- write → **wrote**

Both types are 100% correct Simple Past — irregular verbs aren't wrong or optional, they're just a different pattern. English simply didn't apply the "-ed rule" evenly to all verbs, so a chunk of the most common, everyday verbs (go, eat, see, come, do, have, make, say, get) ended up irregular.

## Why this matters for the exam

Irregular verbs are actually **more likely** to show up as trick options, because the wrong answer often tries to tempt you into adding "-ed" where it doesn't belong (e.g., "goed" instead of "went," "buyed" instead of "bought").
