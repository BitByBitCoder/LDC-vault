# Artificial Intelligence

**Introduction angle:** Frame AI as a technology that is changing work, healthcare, education, and governance, and ask whether it will serve or replace people.

**3 points to build paragraphs around:**

1. **Benefits:** automation of routine work, better healthcare and farming, faster and more personalised education
2. **Challenges:** job displacement, bias and privacy risks, deepfakes and misinformation
3. **Way forward:** regulation, reskilling, keeping humans in control of decisions

**Key vocabulary:** automation, algorithm, machine learning, efficiency, displacement, bias, deepfake, ethical, reskilling, accountability

**Connecting words:** To begin with, Moreover, For instance, As a result, However, Nevertheless, Consequently, In addition, In conclusion

**Tip:** Name real examples (ChatGPT, voice assistants, AI in crop advisory or disease detection, India's IndiaAI Mission). For topics like "Threat or boon", take a clear balanced stand: a boon if used responsibly.

## Model Essay (≈350 words)

Artificial Intelligence has moved from the pages of science fiction into everyday life. From voice assistants on our phones to tools that write, translate, and diagnose, AI is transforming how people work and live. The question is no longer whether AI will shape our future, but whether it will serve humanity or threaten it.

To begin with, the benefits of AI are considerable. It can perform repetitive tasks quickly and accurately, freeing people for more creative work. In healthcare, AI helps doctors detect diseases early through scans and data analysis. In agriculture, it can advise farmers on weather and crop health. Moreover, AI-based learning tools can give students personalised help, even in remote areas. As a result, AI has the potential to improve both productivity and quality of life.

However, AI also brings serious challenges. Automation may replace workers in jobs that involve routine tasks, creating fear of unemployment. In addition, AI systems learn from data, and biased data can lead to unfair decisions. The growing use of deepfakes and AI-generated falsehoods threatens truth and trust, while the collection of personal data raises privacy concerns. Consequently, unchecked AI can harm the very people it is meant to help.

Nevertheless, the way forward lies in responsible use. Governments must frame clear laws to protect privacy and prevent misuse. Schools and training programmes should focus on reskilling workers so that they can work alongside AI rather than be replaced by it. Above all, important decisions affecting human lives must always remain under human supervision.

In conclusion, AI is neither a pure threat nor an automatic blessing. It is a powerful tool whose impact depends on the hands that guide it. If used with wisdom, ethics, and regulation, AI can be a great boon to humanity. If left unchecked, it can become a serious danger. The choice, therefore, rests with us.

## Exam Trap ⚠️

- Don't call AI only "dangerous" or only "wonderful". Examiners reward balance and a clear conclusion.
- Give at least one concrete example (healthcare, farming, deepfakes) instead of general statements.
- Check the word limit: MPSC 2025 asked for 500+ words, while the March 2026 paper asked for about 500. Add one more example paragraph (for instance on education or governance) if you need to stretch it.
