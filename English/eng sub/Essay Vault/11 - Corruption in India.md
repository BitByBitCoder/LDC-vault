## Guide

**Introduction angle:** Define corruption as the misuse of power for personal gain, undermining governance.

**3 Points to build paragraphs around:**
1. Causes — lack of accountability, low transparency, weak enforcement
2. Effects — erosion of public trust, hindered development, inequality
3. Solutions — transparency measures, technology (e-governance), strict accountability

**Key Vocabulary:** malpractice, accountability, transparency, embezzlement, nepotism, governance, integrity

## Model Essay (≈350 words)

Corruption remains one of the most **persistent** challenges to good **governance** in India, referring broadly to the misuse of public power and resources for personal gain at the expense of societal welfare.

**To begin with**, several factors contribute to the persistence of corruption. A lack of **accountability** within institutions, combined with low **transparency** in decision-making processes, creates opportunities for **malpractice**. **Furthermore**, weak enforcement of existing laws often allows corrupt practices, such as bribery and **embezzlement**, to continue unchecked. **In addition**, **nepotism** in public appointments further undermines merit-based governance.

**Moreover**, the effects of corruption are far-reaching. It erodes **public trust** in institutions, discouraging citizen participation in governance. **As a result**, development projects are frequently delayed or compromised, with funds intended for public welfare diverted for private gain. **Consequently**, corruption disproportionately affects the poor, who depend most heavily on public services and have the least ability to navigate corrupt systems — **thus** deepening inequality.

**However**, meaningful solutions do exist. Strengthening **transparency** measures, such as mandatory public disclosure of government spending, can significantly reduce opportunities for misuse. **Notably**, the expansion of **e-governance** and digital transactions — building on initiatives like Digital India — has already reduced opportunities for bribery in several public services by removing human intermediaries. **In addition**, ensuring strict **accountability** through independent oversight bodies is **essential** to sustained reform.

**In conclusion**, corruption undermines both governance and social equity, but it is not an insurmountable challenge. **The way forward lies in** combining technological solutions, institutional transparency, and strict accountability to restore **integrity** in public life and ensure that development reaches all citizens fairly.
