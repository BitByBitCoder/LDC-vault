Here are the three criteria an examiner usually scores. I rated each for difficulty before the fix. The mark split is only an example, so replace it with your exam's actual marks.

## Criterion 1: Grammar (example: 10 marks)

**Difficulty: Medium**

The examiner counts errors. Fewer errors means more marks.

| Weak (loses marks)                                           | Strong (keeps marks)                                           | Error type                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| _Pollution are a big problem. Many peoples suffers from it._ | _Pollution is a big problem. Many people suffer from it._      | Subject-verb agreement, plural form |
| _Yesterday the government pass a law and people are happy._  | _Yesterday the government passed a law and people were happy._ | Tense                               |
| _Its important to protect it's environment._                 | _It is important to protect its environment._                  | its/it's, contraction               |

**How to score high:**

1. Write short, correct sentences rather than long, risky ones.
2. Use rules 1 to 12 from the earlier table as your checklist.
3. Proofread for the verb and its subject in the last 5 minutes.

## Criterion 2: Connection (coherence and cohesion) (example: 10 marks)

**Difficulty: Medium**

The examiner checks whether ideas flow logically, within a paragraph and between paragraphs.

**Weak (ideas are disconnected):**

> _Education is important. Many children cannot attend school. The government should build more schools._

**Strong (ideas are linked):**

> _Education is important for national development. **However**, many children cannot attend school because of poverty. **Therefore**, the government should build more schools._

**Linking words by purpose:**

|Purpose|Words|
|---|---|
|Add an idea|moreover, in addition, furthermore|
|Contrast|however, on the other hand, in contrast|
|Result|therefore, as a result, consequently|
|Example|for instance, for example|
|Conclude|in conclusion, to sum up|

**How to score high:**

1. Start each paragraph with a topic sentence.
2. Use one linking word at the start of each new paragraph.
3. Do not overuse them. One or two per paragraph is enough.
4. Make the conclusion refer back to the introduction.

## Criterion 3: Vocabulary and repeated words (example: 10 marks)

**Difficulty: Hard**

The examiner checks whether you repeat the same word many times. Variety shows a wide vocabulary.

**Weak (repetition):**

> _Pollution is bad. Pollution makes people sick. Pollution also harms animals. We must stop pollution._

**Strong (variety):**

> _Pollution is a serious threat. It makes people ill, harms wildlife, and damages the environment. We must reduce this problem urgently._

**Ways to avoid repeating a word:**

|Technique|Example|
|---|---|
|Use a pronoun|_Pollution is… **It** harms…_|
|Use a synonym|_bad → harmful, damaging, serious_|
|Use a general word|_pollution → this problem, this issue_|
|Combine sentences|_Pollution makes people ill and harms animals._|

**Common overused words and replacements:**

|Overused|Better options|
|---|---|
|good|beneficial, effective, positive|
|bad|harmful, damaging, negative|
|important|essential, significant, vital|
|many|numerous, a large number of|
|show|demonstrate, indicate, reveal|

**How to score high:**

1. Before writing, list 3 to 4 synonyms for your main topic word.
2. Do not use the same word more than twice in one paragraph.
3. Do not use difficult words you are unsure of. A wrong word costs more than a simple word.

## Quick self-check (use in the last 5 minutes)

1. **Grammar:** Is every verb correct for its subject and tense?
2. **Connection:** Does each paragraph start with a linking word or topic sentence?
3. **Repetition:** Did I use any word more than twice in one paragraph?
