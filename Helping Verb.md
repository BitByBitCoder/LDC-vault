It is completely normal to find these plain sentences tricky! When a sentence doesn't have a glaring time word like _yesterday_ or _since 2020_, it feels like looking at a blank wall.

  

Instead of searching for a missing time word, you can solve these mechanical Present Perfect questions using a **two-step formula** that relies purely on grammar rules.

  

### The Two-Step Formula for Present Perfect Options

When you see multiple-choice questions testing _has/have_ + verb forms, run them through these two absolute rules:

  

#### Step 1: Match the Helper Verb (_Has_ vs. _Have_) to the Subject

- **Singular Subject** (_He, She, It, Name_) $\rightarrow$ Must use **has**
    
      
    
- **Plural Subject / I / You** (_They, We, I, You, Children_) $\rightarrow$ Must use **have**_(Instantly cross out options that violate this rule)._
    
      
    

#### Step 2: Check the Main Verb Form (Must be Verb-3)

- The main verb following _has_ or _have_ **must** always be the **Past Participle (Verb-3)** (_completed, gone, eaten_)._(Instantly cross out options that use base verbs like `complete`, `go`, `eat` or wrong past forms like `went`, `ate`)._
    
      
    

### Let's Break Down Your Practice Questions

#### Question 1: She _____ her homework.

- **Subject:** _"She"_ is singular $\rightarrow$ Needs **has** (rules out options _a_ and _d_).
    
      
    
- **Verb Check:** Needs Verb-3 (`completed`) $\rightarrow$ Option _c_ uses `complete` (base form), which is wrong.
    
      
    
- **Winner:** **(b) has completed**
    
      
    

#### Question 2: They _____ to Delhi.

- **Subject:** _"They"_ is plural $\rightarrow$ Needs **have** (rules out options _a_ and _d_).
    
      
    
- **Verb Check:** Needs Verb-3 (`gone`) $\rightarrow$ Option _b_ uses `went` (Verb-2, which cannot follow _have_), which is wrong.
    
      
    
- **Winner:** **(c) have gone**
    
      
    

#### Question 3: He _____ his breakfast.

- **Subject:** _"He"_ is singular $\rightarrow$ Needs **has** (rules out options _a_ and _d_).
    
      
    
- **Verb Check:** Needs Verb-3 (`eaten`) $\rightarrow$ Option _b_ uses `ate` (Verb-2, which cannot follow _has_), which is wrong.
    
      
    
- **Winner:** **(c) has eaten**

Here is a foolproof, formulaic blueprint for tackling these "plain" Present Perfect questions in competitive exams. When time words are missing, you can solve them purely like a math equation by checking two slots.

  

### The Present Perfect Formula

$$\text{Subject} + \Big[ \text{HAS (Singular)} \quad \text{OR} \quad \text{HAVE (Plural / I / You)} \Big] + \text{Verb-3 (Past Participle)}$$

To crack any option safely, your choice **must pass both tests simultaneously**:

  

1. **The Helper Slot Match:** Does the pronoun/noun match _has_ or _have_?
    
      
    
2. **The Action Slot Match:** Is the main verb in its exact **Verb-3** form (_-ed, -en, irregular 3rd form_)?
    
      
    

### Step-by-Step Examples & Traps to Spot

#### Example 1: The Singular Pronoun Test

**Question:** She _____ the report.

  

- (a) have submitted
    
      
    
- (b) has submit
    
      
    
- (c) has submitted
    
      
    
- (d) had submit
    
      
    

**Formula Breakdown:**

  

- **Subject:** _She_ $\rightarrow$ Requires **has** (Eliminate option _a_).
    
      
    
- **Verb Slot:** Must be Verb-3 (_submitted_). Option _b_ and _d_ use _submit_ (base form), which is illegal after _has/had_.
    
      
    
- **Winner:** **(c) has submitted** ($\text{She} + \text{has} + \text{submitted}$).
    
      
    

#### Example 2: The Plural Noun Test

**Question:** The students _____ their exams.

  

- (a) has passed
    
      
    
- (b) have passed
    
      
    
- (c) have pass
    
      
    
- (d) has pass
    
      
    

**Formula Breakdown:**

  

- **Subject:** _The students_ (Plural = They) $\rightarrow$ Requires **have** (Eliminate options _a_ and _d_).
    
      
    
- **Verb Slot:** Must be Verb-3 (_passed_). Option _c_ uses _pass_ (base form).
    
      
    
- **Winner:** **(b) have passed** ($\text{Students} + \text{have} + \text{passed}$).
    
      
    

#### Example 3: The Irregular Verb Trap

**Question:** I _____ my keys.

  

- (a) has lost
    
      
    
- (b) have lose
    
      
    
- (c) have lost
    
      
    
- (d) has losed
    
      
    

**Formula Breakdown:**

  

- **Subject:** _I_ $\rightarrow$ Requires **have** (Eliminate options _a_ and _d_).
    
      
    
- **Verb Slot:** Must be Verb-3. Option _b_ uses _lose_ (base form). _Losed_ in option _d_ is a fake made-up word (the correct irregular Verb-3 of _lose_ is _lost_).
    
      
    
- **Winner:** **(c) have lost** ($\text{I} + \text{have} + \text{lost}$).
