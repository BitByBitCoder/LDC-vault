Good instinct to double-check this — it's a common point of confusion. Let's break down exactly which fraction gets flipped and why.

**The rule: "Keep, Change, Flip"**

When dividing fraction ÷ fraction, only the **second** fraction (the one you're dividing _by_) gets flipped. The first fraction stays exactly as it is.

$$\frac{1}{35} \div \frac{2}{7}$$

- **Keep** the first fraction as-is → $\frac{1}{35}$
- **Change** ÷ to ×
- **Flip** _only_ the second fraction → $\frac{2}{7}$ becomes $\frac{7}{2}$

$$\frac{1}{35} \times \frac{7}{2}$$

**Why not flip both?**

Think about a simple whole-number example first: $10 \div 2 = 5$.

If you rewrite that as a fraction division: $\frac{10}{1} \div \frac{2}{1}$, you already know the answer is 5. Now flip only the second fraction: $\frac{10}{1} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{10}{2} = 5$ ✓ — correct.

But if you flip _both_: $\frac{1}{10} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{20}$ — completely wrong. Flipping both turns your division into something else entirely (it actually gives you $1 \div (10 \times 2)$, not $10 \div 2$).

**The underlying reason:** dividing by a number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal — but only the number you're dividing _by_ gets reciprocated, not the number you started with. $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d}$ is defined as $\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}$ — the $\frac{a}{b}$ never moves.

**Back to your problem, side by side:**

|Wrong (flip both)|Correct (flip only 2nd)|
|---|---|
|$\frac{35}{1} \times \frac{7}{2} = \frac{245}{2}$|$\frac{1}{35} \times \frac{7}{2} = \frac{7}{70} = \frac{1}{10}$|

The correct version gives 10%, which checks out (10% of 2/7 = 1/35). The "flip both" version gives an answer over 100 — a percentage that huge wouldn't even make sense for "what % of 2/7 is a _smaller_ number, 1/35."
